HISTORICAL DECISION-MAKING EXERCISE
JANUARY 1942 — PACIFIC THEATER

The attack on Pearl Harbor has changed the strategic situation in the Pacific. The United States Navy must rapidly adapt to a new form of naval warfare in which aircraft carriers, reconnaissance, fighter protection, surface ships, logistics, and command decisions determine the outcome of major engagements.

You have been assigned command responsibilities within a U.S. Navy carrier task force operating in the Pacific.

SIMULATION OBJECTIVES

RECONNAISSANCE

Interpret intelligence reports and determine what information is most important.

AIR POWER

Manage F4F Wildcats, SBD Dauntlesses, and TBD Devastators.

FORCE PROTECTION

Protect carriers and coordinate cruisers, battleships, and destroyers.

TACTICAL DECISIONS

Evaluate risk, timing, positioning, and offensive opportunities.

LOGISTICS

Manage fuel, ammunition, repairs, and aircraft availability.

COMMAND JUDGMENT

Balance offensive opportunity against the survival of your task force.

EDUCATIONAL USE: This interactive simulation is designed for classroom instruction in World War II military history. Student decisions are evaluated according to historical naval strategy, resource management, force protection, and tactical outcomes.
Historical setting: Pacific Theater, 1942.
This simulation is an educational reconstruction and does not reproduce an actual historical engagement.

    HISTORICAL REFLECTION

    Successful naval commanders had to make decisions with incomplete information. Reconnaissance could be wrong. Aircraft could be lost. Weather could change. Ships could become damaged. Fuel and ammunition were finite. A commander who understood the situation but failed to protect the task force could lose the ability to fight another day.

    Use your experience in this simulation to explain how aircraft carriers, reconnaissance, fighter protection, surface ships, logistics, and command decisions transformed naval warfare in the Pacific.
