JANUARY 1942 — PACIFIC THEATER

Pearl Harbor has changed the strategic situation in the Pacific. Aircraft carriers are becoming the central striking force of naval warfare. The United States Navy needs commanders capable of coordinating aircraft, surface ships, reconnaissance, logistics, and defensive screens.

You will command a 1942 carrier task force through fifteen command phases. Your decisions will affect aircraft availability, ship condition, fuel, ammunition, tactical position, and the ability of your task force to continue operations.

COMMAND OBJECTIVES

RECONNAISSANCE

Use intelligence to determine what the enemy is doing.

AIR POWER

Employ F4F Wildcats, SBD Dauntlesses, and TBD Devastators.

FORCE PROTECTION

Protect the carrier while maintaining offensive capability.

SURFACE FORCES

Coordinate battleships, cruisers, destroyers, and escorts.

LOGISTICS

Manage fuel, ammunition, repairs, and aircraft readiness.

COMMAND JUDGMENT

Balance offensive opportunity against task-force survival.

EDUCATIONAL USE: This interactive historical decision-making simulation is designed for classroom instruction in World War II military history.

    HISTORICAL REFLECTION

    World War II carrier commanders operated with incomplete information. Reconnaissance could be wrong. Aircraft could be lost. Ships could become damaged. Fuel and ammunition were finite. A successful commander had to balance offensive opportunity with the survival of the force.
