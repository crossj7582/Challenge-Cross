1942 CARRIER TASK FORCE DECISION EXERCISE
JANUARY 1942 — PACIFIC THEATER

Pearl Harbor has changed the strategic situation in the Pacific. Aircraft carriers are becoming the central striking force of naval warfare. The United States Navy needs commanders capable of coordinating aircraft, surface ships, reconnaissance, logistics, and defensive screens.

You will command a 1942 carrier task force through fifteen command phases. Your decisions will affect aircraft availability, ship condition, tactical position, and the ability of the task force to continue operations.

COMMAND OBJECTIVES

RECONNAISSANCE

Use intelligence to determine what the enemy is doing.

AIR POWER

Employ F4F Wildcats, SBD Dauntlesses, and TBD Devastators.

FORCE PROTECTION

Protect the carrier while maintaining offensive capability.

SURFACE FORCES

Coordinate battleships, cruisers, destroyers, and escorts.

LOGISTICS

Manage fuel, ammunition, repairs, and aircraft readiness.

COMMAND JUDGMENT

Accept risk when necessary without sacrificing the task force.

EDUCATIONAL USE: This is a classroom historical decision-making simulation. It is designed to reinforce World War II naval strategy, aircraft employment, reconnaissance, logistics, and command judgment.

Historical setting: Pacific Theater, 1942. This is an educational reconstruction, not a reproduction of a specific historical engagement.

    HISTORICAL REFLECTION

    World War II carrier warfare required commanders to operate with incomplete information. Reconnaissance could be incomplete. Aircraft could be lost. Ships could become damaged. Fuel and ammunition were limited. A commander had to decide when to attack, when to maneuver, and when preserving the task force was more important than gaining a temporary advantage.

    Use the decisions made during this simulation to explain how aircraft carriers, naval aviation, reconnaissance, surface ships, logistics, and command judgment changed warfare in the Pacific.
