PACIFIC FLEET COMMAND

WORLD WAR II — PACIFIC THEATRE

MISSION BRIEFING — 1942

Two American carrier strike-force commanders are operating in the same Pacific theatre. Each commander must maneuver ships, establish defensive screens, conduct intelligence reconnaissance, launch sorties, engage enemy surface forces, and preserve combat power.

The battlefield is persistent. Ships move and remain where you put them. Damaged ships remain damaged. Destroyed ships disappear permanently. Aircraft destroyed during sorties are permanently lost.

CARRIER COMMAND: Your aircraft carrier is the center of your strike force. If your carrier reaches 0%, your command ends immediately.

1942 — CARRIER WARFARE

COMMANDER'S BRIEFING

You will fight through a maximum of fifteen rounds. Each commander acts separately. After Commander A acts, the device is passed to Commander B. Commander B sees the battlefield after A's movement before making a decision.

The tactical map shows the enemy force, but that does not mean you know everything about it. Intelligence reconnaissance can reveal aircraft strength, damaged ships, defensive posture, and indications of the enemy's next move. Reconnaissance information can also be incomplete or wrong.

A sortie is an aircraft mission launched from the carrier. Aircraft that return undamaged are restored to the operational tally. Damaged aircraft remain unavailable. Destroyed aircraft are permanently removed.

Surface ships can be selected individually and moved individually. A commander may send part of the surface force forward while leaving the remaining ships around the carrier.
